Customers Are Starting to Ask AI Instead of Googling

By Kevin Hardee · November 5, 2026

For twenty years, finding a local business meant typing a few words into Google. That's still how most people do it. But something is changing, and it's changing fast.

More and more people are skipping the search box and asking an AI tool instead. They type or say something like, "Who's a good fence builder in Muscle Shoals?" and get one answer back, not a list of links.

Let me be clear about the numbers, because they get exaggerated. Google is not going away. By most estimates it still handles the large majority of searches. But ChatGPT alone now handles somewhere between 12 and 19 percent of that volume, depending on who's counting,1 and Google's share has been slipping at its fastest pace in years. A few years ago, that number was close to zero.

I'm not telling you that to scare you. I'm telling you because the direction is clear, and it's moving quickly.

Here's why it matters to a local business. These tools don't invent their answers about your town. They pull from what's already out there: your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your website, and how consistently your name, address and phone number appear across the internet.

That means the same basics that have always helped you get found now do double duty. A correct listing, steady reviews, and a clear website help you show up in Google, and they help an AI tool recommend you by name.

What's different is how the rules are being written. The way Google profiles worked in the past, where being in the top three on the map was the goal, is shifting. Nobody fully knows where it lands yet, and I'd be wary of anyone who says they do.

I follow this closely, because it affects every local business I talk to. What I can tell you is that businesses who start paying attention now will have a head start over the ones who wait until it's obvious.

I can't promise this will bring in customers. What I can say is that getting your information accurate and consistent costs very little, and it's the one thing that helps no matter how people end up searching.
