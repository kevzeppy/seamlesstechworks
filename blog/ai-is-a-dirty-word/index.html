AI Is a Dirty Word. Here's Why It Shouldn't Be.

By Kevin Hardee · November 5, 2026

Say "AI" to a local business owner and watch their face. Some get nervous. Some roll their eyes. A few wonder if a robot is coming for their job.

I get it. The word has been stretched to cover everything from clunky phone menus to headlines about the end of work. If you run a plumbing shop or a restaurant, you have a business to run, and no time for hype.

So let's skip the word and talk about what actually happens in a small business every day.

A customer calls while you're under a sink. You can't answer, and they don't leave a voicemail. They call the next company on the list. That job is gone, and you never knew it existed.

A happy customer says they'll leave a review. They mean it, and then life gets busy. Meanwhile, the competitor down the road has forty reviews and you have six.

Someone checks your hours at nine at night. Google has them wrong. They assume you're closed and move on.

These are small leaks, and they add up. This is where the technology helps, and it looks nothing like the movies.

It looks like a missed call getting a text back in seconds: "Sorry we missed you, how can we help?" The customer replies, and you pick it up when you're out from under the sink.

It looks like a review request going out after a job, with a simple link, so a happy customer can say so in thirty seconds.

It looks like your Google listing staying accurate, so people find the right hours, phone number and address.

That's it. It's the follow-up you'd do yourself if you had four more hands.

What it doesn't do is replace you. Customers hire you because you know your trade, you show up, and you stand behind your work. No software does that. It just keeps you from losing people before you get the chance.

There's a fair reason owners are cautious. A lot of what's sold under this label is overpriced, overpromised, or built for a company ten times your size. If someone can't explain in plain words what it will do for your shop, walk away.

Here's my test. Can it answer a missed call? Can it ask for a review? Can it keep your information right? If it can't point to a customer you would have lost without it, skip it.
